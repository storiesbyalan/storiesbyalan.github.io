EPISODE 001

LEBANON — RAFIK AL HARIRI AIRPORT

It’s midday.

The harsh summer sun blazes down calling forth heat shimmers.

Traffic and the sound of horns pollute the entrance to the airport. Taxi drivers stand with their backs laid on their cars waving down arrivals asking whether or not those walking out of the airport need a ride.

Some take their offer. Others shake their heads or raise their palms. A clear rejection.

Amir Haddad walks out of the airport. He was 27 when he left Lebanon. Three years and a military haircut later, Amir returns with a backpack and a big duffle bag. He stops and stands as other arrivals walk past him; sets the bag between his feet, and pulls out his phone.

Amir dials a number then brings the mobile to his ear.

AMIR: Hey, it’s me… Yeah, I just landed… Thanks… I’ll make sure to be there bright and early tomorrow morning… Yeah… See you then… Bye.

A crooked smile appears on his face.

He puts his mobile back in his pocket, picks up the duffle bag.

AMIR: Here goes nothing.

Amir chuckles, shaking his head.

BEIRUT — J&R STUDIOS — JAD’S OFFICE

Jad Njeim sits behind his desk, facing the window. His piercing blue eyes scan the horizon. He’s an unconventionally handsome 60-year-old man with curly graying hair and a matching trimmed beard. Despite being the CEO and co-founder of J&R Studios, he wears a sky blue button-down shirt with rolled up sleeves, and fitted black jeans.

Jad’s office is minimalistic, organized: only a few posters hang on the walls near a small shelf with a couple of books. A laptop sits on the desk, next to it is a stack of scripts, Jad’s mobile phone, and a family portrait in a simple black frame.

A young man sits in the chair across from Jad’s desk. His knee bobs up and down.

YOUNG MAN: Dad?

Jad does not respond. His eyes still enraptured by the world beyond his office. His mind working through every possible outcome the next conversation is going to have.

This wasn't personal. It was business.

YOUNG MAN: Dad, is everything ok?

Jad snaps out of his daze and turns to face his son. He lays his elbows on the desk and interlocks his fingers.

JAD: We’re not at a good place, JJ. The company… is not at a good place. “Until Morning,” barely recouped its budget, despite every promise you made ensuring me that it would be a success.

JJ closes his eyes. Takes a deep breath.

The moment he's been dreading finally here.

After what seems like his own solitary eternity, he opens his eyes and looks directly at his father, who bears a stern expression.

JJ looks young for his age, newly 30. He’s a mix of his mother and father. Structurally, he has his dad’s curly hair and sharp features, though JJ’s are a touch softer. His hazel eyes, however, are a carbon copy of his mother’s. JJ dons an auburn mullet, and an earring in each ear.

JJ: Dad, I -

JAD: No excuses, JJ. You greenlit the film despite all my concerns. I told you productions like this might put the entire company at risk, and here we are facing the consequences. We have to find a way to make up for what we lost.

JJ: It was a solid film. I believed in it and the director.

JJ slightly raises his voice, the seed of frustration breaking open. Within mere seconds, he composes himself. Despite Jad being his father, he was and is the CEO of J&R Studios, sits before him.

JJ: Critics loved it. Many said it has potential to be Lebanon’s submission for next year’s Oscars. I know you know that counts for something.

JAD: Critics and the Oscars don’t matter if we’re not making money, JJ.

JJ: Now you sound like him.

Jad glares at his son.

JJ cowers back in his chair. If there was one line he shouldn't have crossed it was this one. Much like he'd done before, the rage inside his dad dissipates. 

JAD: You know damn well that I’m nothing like that man. I pride myself for being a supporter of independent Lebanese filmmakers, but there needs to be a balance. We can’t alienate our audience.

JJ: But dad-

Jad raises his palm cutting JJ off.

JAD: If you can’t find a way to pivot or build a stricter vetting process, then I’ll find someone else who will.

JJ furrows his brow in disbelief: was his father threatening him? Was there something else going on?

BEIRUT — HADDAD MEDIA — CONFERENCE ROOM

The conference room is narrow and long, with a rectangular table in the center. Glass walls encompass it, with one side overlooking the city.

George Haddad, a typical company man in a three-piece suit, sits at the head of the table. George’s slicked back salt and pepper hair and tidy gray beard highlight his stern eyes.

Despite holding a domineering and intimidating energy about him, the creases in his forehead and slightly dark circles around his eyes are evidence of how hard the journey has been.

George is the CEO and founder of Haddad Media, an entertainment media empire. He inherited the company from his father 40 years ago. It was nothing but a chain of movie theaters back then, and he was only 20, when he had to take over.

To George’s right sits his son Marwan, a dapper soon-to-be 35-year-old James Bond type, with a strong aura much like his father’s. Across from Marwan sits Sasha, his sister. At 25, she’s the youngest yet one of the most fearless at the table. She dons a pixie haircut with platinum tips, and wears a bold indigo pant suit.

Other board members fill the remaining seats at the table.

GEORGE: Where are we in terms of post-production on the film?

MARWAN: Last I checked they’re finalizing coloring after adding in all the SFX and CGI.

SASHA: Will we have enough to release a trailer and begin marketing the premiere? I want us to be able to get that going as soon as possible.

MARWAN: I can ask, but I’m certain that shouldn’t be a problem.

One of the board members, Youssef, an older man around the age of George, perhaps a bit older, clears his throat as he leans onto the table.

YOUSSEF: What are the projections in terms of the box office? How confident are we that this is going to give us a good return? We need a hit, especially after J&R’s latest flop.

GEORGE: Don’t worry about that Youssef, we are quite confident. The film stars four A-Listers: two from here, and two from Syria. There’s no doubt it’s going to be a summer blockbuster.

Lilliane, the only other woman aside from Sasha, sits up in her chair. Clears her throat making space for her to speak.

LILLIANE: While I appreciate your confidence, movie theater attendance hasn’t been the greatest, especially since COVID. Our concern is justifiable, George.

GEORGE: Your concerns are valid, but again, as I told you, we are very confident that this film is going to bring people back to movie theaters.

YOUSSEF: But you know as well as anyone that there’s never a guarantee in this business, and -

Marwan cuts Youssef off. Voice stern.

MARWAN: With all due respect, you haven’t seen what I’ve had the privilege to see. The film is unlike anything this country has seen before.

A tense silence fills the room. George surveils the room, then leans forward, placing his elbows on the table and interlocking his fingers, his domineering aura pulsing throughout the room.

GEORGE: Are we good to move on to discussing the premiere?

Intimidated, Youssef and Lilliane nod. They fall back in line with George.

GEORGE: Good.

DOWNTOWN BEIRUT — AMIR’S APARTMEN 

Amir opens the door to his apartment.

Exhales, exhaustion washing over him.

Traffic was a killer.

He flicks the light switch on. The apartment lights flicker for a moment before stabilizing.

The apartment is just as he left it three years ago: a living, breathing collage of bohemian and antique Lebanese treasures. Each piece embracing its own history, like they’d traveled a long way to end up right here in this space.

The only difference is how dusty it’s gotten. The wooden coffee table, worn and nicked from years of being handed down and sold off, sits near an old armchair with faded embroidery.

AMIR: Home sweet home.

Amir’s smile fights back against his exhausted state.

AMIR: Good to be back.

Amir closes the door and walks to his bedroom, his bags forcing him to squeeze through the threshold. He drops them near the bed, grabs his mobile and starts playing the latest Stray Kids album. Nothing like feel good K-Pop music.

Amir unzips the duffle bag. Lays his clothes on the bed in small vertical piles, singing along and dancing to the music.

A knock on the door interrupts him.

He pauses the music. Furrows his brow.

Strange nobody was supposed to know he was back.

Amir reaches the door.

AMIR: Who is it?

WOMAN’S VOICE: Bitch, open the door.

Amir immediately rolls his eyes. Opens the door.

If her demand wasn’t recognizable enough, her appearance definitely was.

Bushy curly hair, three earrings on one ear and one hanging on the other. Eclectic clothing attires that were taken straight of the 1960’s. A whiskey bottle in her right hand.

AMIR: How the hell did you know I’m back, Samira?

Samira slightly opens her mouth, glaring at Amir in utter disbelief.

J&R STUDIOS — JAD’S OFFICE

JJ leans forward towards Jad’s desk, brow still furrowed.

JJ: What the hell is that supposed to mean?

JAD: You know exactly what it means. The risk we took cost us more than it should have. We can’t wait around while our competition overtakes what we’ve been trying so hard to achieve. You need to fix this.

JJ: So, that’s it? It’s all about the competition?

Jad slams his palm on the desk.

JAD: Of course it’s about the competition! What the hell do you think this business is all about, son?

JJ: When you established this department for me, this expansion on J&R Studios, we both knew I wanted to focus more on the artistic aspects of the industry. To go back to the basics. Deliver films that didn’t bend the knee to the market. That focused on the storytelling not the spectacle.

JAD: JJ, I understand that, but something’s got to give. You can’t expect the company board to accept losing on investment.

JJ shakes his head. Frustration boiling inside him.

JJ: Well then, what do you propose we do? Shut down the department?!

JAD: Of course not. But I think we need to reevaluate, and perhaps even take it in a new direction. Perhaps even bring in someone who can help you do that.

JJ: What? Dad, I can do this on my own.

JAD: JJ, I don’t think you can. You’re too close to this and we need someone who can help ground you when your idealism gets in the way.

JJ tilts his head down, and stares at the floor beneath him, visibly hurt.

JJ: So, you don’t trust me…

JAD: It’s not a matter of trust. It’s a matter of wanting you and this department to reach its full potential. The best way to do that, is to bring in someone who can help.

JJ shakes his head, then closes his eyes. He knew that this wasn't going to be easy, but he didn't expect this. Didn't expect Jad to throw away the towel so quickly.

JJ: You’re not even giving the film a chance. Word-of-mouth will give you what you want. Can we please give it time before we do anything?

JAD: We need a win, JJ. Haddad Media is planning their next release, and I’m certain they’re going to go out of their way to upstage us, and use the situation we are in to their advantage. We can’t let them do that. We have to get ahead of it.

JJ: And this is how you want to do it? Cripple my vision and bring in some stranger?

JAD: Who said anything about them being a stranger?

JJ’s brow arches. Stares at his father. His eyes quizzical with a dash of worry. 

AMIR’S APARTMENT

Samira shakes her head and pushes past Amir into the apartment.

SAMIRA: Usually if your best friend decides to come back home, they’d let you know beforehand. I guess that doesn’t apply to us?

AMIR: You didn’t answer my question…

Amir closes the door.

SAMIRA: You didn’t answer mine.

Samira sets the whiskey bottle on the table in the living room.

AMIR: Samira...

SAMIRA: Amir…

Amir narrows his eyes. He knows Samira’s mocking tone all too well.

AMIR: What are you doing here?

SAMIRA: Why didn’t you tell me you were coming back?

AMIR: I didn’t tell anyone I was coming back.

SAMIRA: I’m not anyone.

Samira crosses her arms.

Amir sighs, then softly smiles.

A smile sprinkled with guilt.

AMIR: Ok, you’re right. I’m sorry. I should’ve told you, but honestly it happened really fast. I wasn’t expecting to come back, hell I wasn’t even thinking about it after everything that happened with my family.

SAMIRA: Have you seen them yet?

AMIR: I’m sure they’re busy preparing for some new release or something. You know how dad is.

SAMIRA: What about your mom?

Amir pauses. Looks away from Samira.

His mom was sore spot. Any mention of her always stung.

AMIR: No, I haven’t. Not sure I will.

SAMIRA: She was devastated when you left, you know that right?

AMIR: She sure had a way of showing it. Anyway, can we change the subject?
Samira nods.

SAMIRA: Drink?

AMIR: God, yes. You know where the cups are.

Samira walks to the kitchen. Grabs two glasses, then returns to the living room and hands Amir a glass. Whiskey bottle open, she pours into Amir's glass then hers.

Samira settles next to him on the couch.

SAMIRA: So, are you gonna tell me what brought you back, or do I have to pull it out of you?

Amir is about to answer, when his mobile rings.

AMIR: One sec. I have to take this.

Samira raises an eyebrow as he answers the call.

AMIR: Hello? Yes, this is he… Right now? Sure, no problem at all… You can tell him that I’ll be there as soon as possible… See you then.

Amir hangs up the call. His eyes meet Samira’s inquisitive eyes.

SAMIRA: Amir… What aren’t you telling me?

Amir, still holding Samira’s gaze, slowly smirks.

HADDAD MEDIA — GEORGE’S OFFICE

George sits at his desk, elbow on his chair’s armrest. He massages his forehead just above the brow. Marwan walks into the office and immediately notices, a look of concern washing over his face.

MARWAN: Dad, you ok? I can come back later.

GEORGE: No, it’s alright. Just a minor headache.

George lifts his head and meets his son’s eyes.

GEORGE: What’s up?

MARWAN: I wanted to check back in with you about the meeting earlier. Is the board going to be a problem?

GEORGE: I don’t suspect they will, and if I get a whiff that it might head in that direction, I’ll make sure to take care of it.

Marwan nods, as George turns towards the window, almost in a daze.

MARWAN: You’re sure you’re ok?

GEORGE: I’m fine.

MARWAN: Maybe you should just head home. I can hold down the fort for the rest of the day. I’m sure it’ll make mom happy.

GEORGE: Maybe I will. I’ll let you know if I do.

MARWAN: Alright.

Marwan turns towards the door and opens it. He looks back. George, once again, stares out the window. Marwan opens the door leaving George alone in the office.

He closes the door behind him. Sighs. Worry clear as day on his face.

J&R STUDIOS — JAD’S OFFICE

JJ stands. Paces, frustration boiling over.

JAD: I never said I’m bringing in a stranger. In fact, I think you two will work wonderfully together.

JJ stops behind the chair he was sitting on only moments before.

JJ: What are you talking about?

JAD: Look you’re just going to have to trust me. I’m doing what’s best for J&R Studios.

JJ: Why didn’t you discuss this with me first?

JAD: Discuss it with you?

Jad raises his voice, matching JJ's frustration.

JAD: Need I remind you that I’m the CEO of J&R Studios. I don’t have to discuss anything with you. Even if it has to do with your department.

He takes a deep breath. Gathers himself. Last thing he wanted was a scene.

JAD: I’ve made my decision and it’s final.

JJ: Dad -

JAD: Enough, JJ! Either calmly sit back down, or you can go back to your office.

JJ shakes his head. Turns towards the door, reaching out to the handle.

He pauses, then turns to face his father.

JJ: You know dad, the day you approved my pitch for the Indie Film & Story Development department… it was one of the best days of my life. I’m sorry if I let you down.

JAD: You haven’t let me down, son. Nothing I'm doing here is meant to express that. But this is what's best for the company. It's not personal.

JJ: So, I'm not supposed to take you sidelining personally?

JAD: For the hundredth time JJ, I’m not sidelining you. I’m bringing in someone who is going to help you. Work beside you. Co-captain this ship of yours so that it doesn’t drown and take the entire company down with it.

JJ: Well, let’s just hope we can trust whoever they are enough to do that.

A knock on the door sounds.

JJ steps aside. Opens the door, eyes widening.

Out of all the people who JJ's crossed paths with, he didn't expect him to be standing on the other side of that door.

JAD: Perfect timing, we were just talking about you.

JJ glares his father. His face red boils with rage.

He turns to meet the eyes of the person standing in front of him:

Amir.

END OF EPISODE 001

MARWAN: I’m worried about dad. I can’t put my finger on it, but I know something’s off.

Sasha tilts her head, concern evident on her face.

J&R STUDIOS — MARKETING DEPARTMENT

Jess (JJ’s sister) and a man her age scroll through social media. Negative criticism and hate consuming the online discourse.

JESS: Damn it.

MAN: This isn’t going to help your brother’s cause is it?

J&R STUDIOS — JAD’S OFFICE

Jad smiles as he addresses Amir.

JAD: You’ll be working alongside JJ, helping him fine-tune and reassess the department I set up for him.

JJ: Like hell he will.
